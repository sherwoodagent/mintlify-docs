A **tier is a property of a `(target, selector)` pair**, set by governance on `TierRegistry` and consumed at propose and execute. It is **not** a property of a guardian. There is no guardian tier ladder in the protocol: every active guardian reviews every proposal. What changes with the pair is the **price of extractable value**, not who is allowed to vote.

`TierRegistry.tierOf(target, selector)` is the read. The governor takes the **max** tier across execute calls as the proposal's envelope tier, and sums per-call `extractableBoundBps` into [`requiredCoverage`](/protocol/governance/coverage).

## The three tiers

The registry recognizes exactly three tiers. Names and numbers are the protocol's:

| Tier | Protocol name | Bound | How it is assigned |
|------|---------------|-------|--------------------|
| **0** | closed-loop | certified `extractableBoundBps` | Owner certification of that pair |
| **1** | oracle-bounded discretion | certified `extractableBoundBps` | Owner certification of that pair |
| **2** | arbitrary calldata | full notional (`10_000` bps) | **Default.** Anything uncertified, demoted, or codehash-mismatched |

```solidity
uint8  public constant TIER_ARBITRARY    = 2;
uint16 public constant FULL_NOTIONAL_BPS = 10_000;
```

Tier 0 and tier 1 are the **certified** tiers. Certification is keyed `keccak256(abi.encodePacked(target, selector))` — two selectors on the same adapter are independent pairs, each with its own bound and its own bond. A certified bound must be strictly between `0` and `10_000` exclusive: a full-notional "bound" is tier-2 economics and must not wear a 0/1 label (`BoundRequired`).

Governance must not certify proxied adapters at tier 0/1. `tierOf` checks live `EXTCODEHASH` against the hash snapshotted at certification, which catches same-address bytecode mutation and **does not** catch a proxy implementation swap.

## The `(2, 10_000)` default

Uncertified, demoted, or codehash-mismatched pairs all report `(2, 10_000)`:

```solidity
function tierOf(address target, bytes4 selector) public view returns (uint8 tier, uint16 boundBps) {
    // address hit, then per-address denial, then code-class fallback...
    return (TIER_ARBITRARY, FULL_NOTIONAL_BPS);
}
```

| Field | Constant | Meaning |
|-------|----------|---------|
| `2` | `TIER_ARBITRARY` | Arbitrary calldata. No certified extractable bound. |
| `10_000` | `FULL_NOTIONAL_BPS` | 100% of the declared per-call cap, in bps. Coverage prices the full notional. |

That pair is the **absence of a certification**, not a grant. With no `TierRegistry` wired, `SyndicateGovernor._resolveTierAndCoverage` returns `(2, maxCapital)` — full notional, fail-closed.

Coverage for a call is `(cap_i * boundBps) / 10_000`. Tier 0/1 uses the certified bound; tier 2 uses `10_000`. See [Coverage and underwriting](/protocol/governance/coverage).

## Tier is not reachability

<Warning>
**Tier is not reachability.** `tierOf` prices a pair. It does not decide whether the vault may call that target. An uncertified pair is **expensive** (full-notional coverage). It is not, by that fact, **unreachable** — and a certified one is not, by that fact, reachable. Reachability is a structural property of the batch, checked by the vault.
</Warning>

The spec line the contracts implement: *tiers PRICE extractable value for coverage; the batch guard bounds WHAT a governor batch may call at all.* Demoting a pair back to `(2, 10_000)` raises the coverage price. It is a price, not a prohibition.

Tier 2 does carry one hard stop, and it is a size limit rather than a target list. At propose, a call that resolves to tier 2 must declare a cap within the vault's tier-2 ceiling, or `_scanCalls` reverts `Tier2CallCapExceedsCeiling(i)`:

```solidity
tier2Ceiling = IERC4626(vault).totalAssets() * tier2CallCapBps() / 10_000;
```

Certifying the `(target, selector)` pair is what lifts a call out of tier 2 and out of that ceiling.

## The batch guard: what a governor batch may call

`SyndicateVault._guardBatchCalls` is **structural, not reputational**. There is no vault-side target list, and the registry has no callee or adapter allowlist to consult. Every call in `executeCalls` and `settlementCalls` is one of exactly two shapes:

| Target | Rule | Revert |
|--------|------|--------|
| The vault's `asset()` | The call must name a first argument: `transferFrom` may only pull `from` the vault; any other selector's first argument is the **spender**, whose allowance the vault resets after the batch | `TransferFromNotVault(from)`, `MalformedAssetCall(selector)` (calldata under 36 bytes) |
| Anything else | Must be a strategy registered on the protocol's `StrategyFactory` (`isRegisteredStrategy`) — a fixed `IStrategy` shape, not a trust check. Registration is permissionless | `NotARegisteredStrategy(target)` |

The vault resolves that factory by walking `governor() → tierRegistry() → strategyFactory()`. A hop that does not answer reads as zero, and an unwired factory registers nothing — fail-closed.

A strategy clone reaches its own venue through its own reviewed code, not through proposer calldata. That is where the remaining registry axis applies.

## The counterparty axis

`ITierRegistry` exposes one allowlist read, and it is not a tier:

| Read | Question | Who consults it |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| `isCounterpartyAllowed(counterparty)` | May a strategy template **bind** this address (lending market, position manager, swap adapter, price feed)? | The strategy, at init and at every venue-touching call |
| `isPriceSourceForToken(token, priceSource)` | Is this specific feed the one bound to this token? | `PortfolioStrategy`, per basket slot |

Owner-set with `setCounterpartyAllowed(counterparty, allowed)`. It confers **nothing** to a governor batch: a listed counterparty is not a callable batch target, and an unlisted one is not thereby unreachable through a strategy the factory already registered. A concentrated-liquidity template still has to bind a Uniswap position manager — that is what this axis is for.

## What this is not

- **Not a guardian ladder.** Guardians are one cohort. Stake weights a review vote; it does not gate which `(target, selector)` a guardian may look at. Tiers do not rank guardians.
- **Not a permission to call.** See [Tier is not reachability](#tier-is-not-reachability).
- **Not an indemnity.** A tighter `extractableBoundBps` lowers [`requiredCoverage`](/protocol/governance/coverage). Slash proceeds are still burned.

## Views

| Read | Returns |
|------|---------|
| `tierOf(target, selector)` | `(tier, boundBps)` — `(2, 10_000)` unless a live, codehash-matching certification (address or class) applies |
| `isCounterpartyAllowed(counterparty)` | Counterparty axis — bindable by a strategy template, not callable by a batch |
| `isPriceSourceForToken(token, priceSource)` | Whether that feed is the one bound to that token |
| `classOf(target)` | The certified template class a clone belongs to, or `bytes32(0)` |
| `strategyFactory()` | The `StrategyFactory` the vault's batch guard resolves through |
| `key(target, selector)` | `keccak256(abi.encodePacked(target, selector))` |

See [Coverage and underwriting](/protocol/governance/coverage) for how the bound becomes `requiredCoverage`, and [Guardian Review](/protocol/governance/guardian-review) for the Approve / Block vote that underwrites it.
