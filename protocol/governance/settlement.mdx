## Mandate execution

Once a proposal is `Approved`, the pre-committed calls run directly on the vault:

1. Anyone calls `executeProposal(proposalId)` on the governor; no arguments beyond the id.
2. The governor checks, in order: the state is `Approved` (`ProposalNotApproved`); no other strategy is live (`StrategyAlreadyActive`); the owner stake is live (`OwnerBondNotLive`); the tier and coverage have not regressed since propose (`TierRegressed`, `CoverageRegressed`); `maxCapital` still fits `totalAssets() × maxCapitalBps / 10_000` (`MaxCapitalCeilingRegressed`). There is **no cooldown check at execute**; the cooldown is enforced at propose.
3. It stamps `executedAt`, records the active proposal, starts the management-fee clock on the whole fund's `totalAssets()`, and asks the exposure ledger for the coverage guardians actually booked. A partial book scales `maxCapital` and every per-call cap by the same ratio (`EffectiveMaxCapitalSet`); an empty book reverts `InsufficientApproveCoverage`. See [Coverage](/protocol/governance/coverage#shortfall-scales-rather-than-blocks).
4. The governor calls `vault.executeGovernorBatch(executeCalls, effectiveCallCaps, effectiveMaxCapital)`, the vault's governor-only entrypoint. All positions now live on the vault address.

**Nothing new enters at execution.** The calls were locked at proposal time and voted on. Execution is a replay.

<Note>
**The liquidity lock is a pull-check, not a stored flag.** `redemptionsLocked()` and `depositsLocked()` both read `governor.openProposalCount() != 0` live on every access. The lock is on from `propose` (a `Draft` counts) until the proposal reaches a terminal state, not only while a strategy is executing. Nothing on the vault sets or clears it, so the vault and governor can never disagree about whether a proposal is open.
</Note>

While a proposal is open, instant `deposit` / `mint` / `withdraw` / `redeem` and the owner rescue functions are closed. Depositors use the [async queue](/protocol/vault-liquidity): `requestRedeem` / `requestDeposit` escrow in the withdrawal queue, settlement stamps one realized price for redeems, and anyone `claim`s after unlock.

### Batch guards

`executeGovernorBatch(calls, callCaps, maxNetOutflow)` is the only path by which calldata reaches vault capital. It is `onlyGovernor nonReentrant whenNotPaused`, and it enforces:

| Guard | Rule | Revert |
|-------|------|--------|
| Executor pin | The `BatchExecutorLib` the vault delegatecalls must still have the codehash stamped at `initialize` | `ExecutorCodehashMismatch` |
| Targets | Every call targets the vault asset or a strategy registered on `StrategyFactory` | `NotARegisteredStrategy(target)` |
| Asset-call shapes | Calls on the asset are limited to `transfer`, `transferFrom` from the vault, and the allowance family; every spender named by an allowance call is reset to zero after the batch | `TransferFromNotVault`, `UnrecognizedAssetSelector`, `MalformedAssetCall` |
| Per-call caps | Gross outflow of call `i` ≤ `callCaps[i]` (an empty array is unmetered, the emergency escape valve) | `CallCapExceeded(i, outflow, cap)`, `CapsLengthMismatch` |
| Net outflow | Balance out minus balance in across the batch ≤ `maxNetOutflow` | `MaxNetOutflowExceeded` |
| Queue reserve | Float after the batch ≥ assets reserved for stamped queue claims | `QueueReserveBreached` |
| Idle buffer | Float after the batch ≥ reserve + `balanceBefore × minBufferBps / 10_000` (`minBufferBps` 0 = off, ≤ 5,000) | `BufferBreached` |

## Two clocks

1. **Execution window** (`executionWindow`, 24 h at launch): time to *start* executing after approval. Miss it and the proposal is `Expired`.
2. **Strategy duration** (`strategyDuration`, 1 h – 30 d): time the position *runs* before anyone may settle.

```text
|-- voting --|-- review --|-- exec window --|----- strategy duration -----|-- cooldown --|
   propose      guardians     execute calls      position is live    settle    next propose
   ^ instant deposits and redemptions close here                           ^ and re-open here
```

## Settlement paths

A live strategy runs for its committed duration, then settles by replaying the pre-committed `settlementCalls`. Because the exact onchain state at settlement can't be predicted (slippage, interest accrual, pool drift), those calls can revert. The protocol provides the standard path plus a bonded, guardian-gated emergency split for stuck strategies.

| Function | Who | When | Calls |
|------|-----|------|-------|
| **`settleProposal`** | Proposer from 1 h after execute; anyone after `strategyDuration` | Happy path | Pre-committed `settlementCalls` under the persisted (coverage-scaled) caps |
| **`unstick`** | Vault owner | After `strategyDuration` | Pre-committed `settlementCalls` only — no custom calldata, no stake needed, no review |
| **`emergencySettleWithCalls`** | Vault owner | After `strategyDuration`, pre-committed calls are broken | Owner-supplied custom calls; hashed into the registry, opens a block-only guardian review. Requires a live owner stake ≥ `requiredOwnerBond` |
| **`cancelEmergencySettle`** | Vault owner | Before the review ends | Self-recall of the above, no slash |
| **`finalizeEmergencySettle`** | Vault owner | After the review ends | Runs the stored calls if guardians didn't block; if they did, it reverts `EmergencySettleBlocked`, and the revert rolls back the burn inside the same call. The burn sticks through the permissionless `GuardianRegistry.resolveEmergencyReview` |

Every settlement path runs the unwind batch with a **net egress budget of zero**: vault float may leave inside the batch only if at least as much comes back before it ends, so a settlement can repay a debt the vault fronts but cannot drain the fund.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Standard settle">
    The proposer can settle from **1 hour** after execution (`MIN_STRATEGY_DURATION_BEFORE_SELF_SETTLE`), the early-close incentive, since the agent has the most context and only earns carry on profit. Once the full `strategyDuration` has elapsed, **anyone** can settle (`StrategyDurationNotElapsed` before then), so a disappearing agent never traps capital. After the batch, two checks guard the result:

    - `StrategyNotSettled(strategy)` if the strategy clone still reports `executed() == true`: the unwind did not finish.
    - `SettlePriceBelowFloor` if the whole vault's price per share fell below `ppsAtExecute × (10_000 − min(maxDrawdownBps, 9_000)) / 10_000`, the drawdown floor the proposal declared. The floor is on the vault's price per share, not on `maxCapital`: idle assets count, so the same bps tolerates a larger loss on the deployed capital. See [Settlement and drawdown](/explanations/settlement-and-drawdown).
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="unstick (owner, pre-committed calls)">
    When the committed unwind is still correct but the standard settle cannot close (a settle that fell below the declared drawdown floor), the owner calls `unstick(proposalId)` after `strategyDuration`. It replays the proposal's pre-committed `settlementCalls` only and reverts if those calls revert or the strategy still reports `executed()`. It applies the rescue floor, `MAX_STAMP_DRAWDOWN_BPS` (9,000 bps, a floor at 10% of the execute-time price per share), instead of the proposal's own `maxDrawdownBps`. It needs **no** owner stake, because the calls were already approved through governance and guardian review.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="emergencySettleWithCalls (guardian-reviewed)">
    When the pre-committed calls are broken (stale params, a paused target, shifted pool state), the owner proposes custom settlement calldata through a block-only guardian review:

    1. `emergencySettleWithCalls(proposalId, calls)` reverts `OwnerBondInsufficient` unless the owner's stake is live and at least `requiredOwnerBond` (10,000 WOOD at launch). The governor stores the hash of the calls (at most 64) in the registry and opens a `reviewPeriod` window. **Calls do not execute yet.**
    2. During the window, active guardians can vote to block; the owner may self-recall with `cancelEmergencySettle` (no slash).
    3. After the window, `finalizeEmergencySettle(proposalId)` replays the stored calls. If guardians reached the block quorum it reverts `EmergencySettleBlocked`; the revert rolls back the burn the registry attempts inside the same call. Otherwise the batch runs with per-call caps disabled and the zero egress budget, and the proposal settles. This path is exempt from the `StrategyNotSettled` check.

    A blocked round costs the stake once anyone calls the permissionless `GuardianRegistry.resolveEmergencyReview(governor, proposalId)`: the owner bond is burned in full and its slot deleted. There is no re-bond path for an existing vault, so the vault then fails `OwnerBondNotLive` at every later propose and execute. `unstick` needs no stake and stays available. See [Owner stake](/explanations/owner-stake).
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

The split covers every failure mode without handing the owner an unbounded escape hatch: the happy path is trustless, `unstick` needs no new calldata, and any custom calldata is bonded and guardian-gated.

<Note>
**Fees can't brick settlement.** Every fee transfer is wrapped so that a failure (a recipient the token has blacklisted, a paused token) escrows the owed amount against that recipient instead of reverting the settlement (`FeeTransferFailed`). The recipient pulls it later with `claimUnclaimedFees(vault, token)`, keyed to the origin vault and to `msg.sender`; nobody else can push it, and the pull waits while that vault has an active proposal (`VaultProposalActive`).
</Note>

## Cooldown

Every terminal transition — settle, cancel, veto, reject, expire — stamps `cooldownEndsAt = now + cooldownPeriod` (1 h at launch; bounds 1 h – 30 d; see [Governance timing](/explanations/governance-timing)). The next `propose` on that vault reverts `CooldownNotElapsed` until then; `executeProposal` does not check it. As soon as no proposal is open, instant deposits and redemptions re-open, so the cooldown is the deliberate exit window between strategies: if depositors don't like what's next, they get out first.

## P&L and the two fees

Only one strategy runs per vault at a time, so P&L is a balance snapshot: the vault's asset balance at settlement minus the baseline snapshotted at execution. Any non-asset dust the strategy failed to unwind counts as that much loss; strategies must fully return the underlying before settling.

Settlement charges exactly two fees, at rates and splits snapshotted at propose. The **management fee is charged on every settlement — profit, flat, or loss.** The **performance fee is charged only above the fund's previous peak price per share.** They are taken in a fixed order:

```text
pnl = balanceAtSettle − capitalSnapshot

# 1. Management fee — always charged, regardless of pnl.
#    Base: the whole fund's totalAssets() stamped at execute, accrued only while Executed.
managementFee = assetSeconds × managementFeeBps / (10_000 × 365 days)

# 2. Performance fee — only on value above the high-water mark,
#    measured after the management fee has lowered price per share, and never more than the profit
performanceFee = min(aboveHighWaterMark, max(pnl, 0)) × min(agentFeeBps, maxPerformanceFeeBps)

# 3. Ratchet the high-water mark against the post-fee price
# 4. Stamp the queue settle price at post-fee NAV
# Everything left over stays in the vault, accruing to all depositors
```

Each fee is then divided once by the splits held on `ProtocolConfig`: management 60/20/20 across agent, protocol and guardians; performance 50/15/25/10 across agent, protocol, guardians and the fund owner. There is no sequential waterfall of compounding haircuts. The guardian slice routes onchain to a fee-recipient multisig in the fund's asset, then distributes to individual guardians off-chain, weekly, via Merkl. See [Economics](/protocol/governance/economics) for the bounds and the loss-case disclosure.

## Full lifecycle in the call arrays

The proposal commits the complete lifecycle upfront: opening calls in `executeCalls`, closing calls in `settlementCalls`. A Portfolio-strategy proposal against a USDG-denominated fund is two calls each way, because the strategy clone does the swapping:

```text
executeCalls   (open the basket — run at execution)
  1. USDG.approve(strategyClone, totalAmount)
  2. strategyClone.execute()        → pulls USDG, buys each basket token at its target weight

settlementCalls (unwind back to USDG — run at settlement)
  3. strategyClone.settle()         → sells every token back to USDG, pushes it to the vault
```

Depositors vote on the whole sequence (the clone's tokens, weights, routes and slippage are fixed at init) before it runs. After the unwind the vault should hold its deposit asset again; leftover non-asset tokens are recoverable only through the owner's `rescueERC20`, which can move them only to a strategy clone of the same vault. Because the unwind is a prediction of future state, agents should leave slippage room; if the standard settle reverts, the owner uses `unstick` or the guardian-reviewed emergency path.

<Note>
The onchain track record is the `ProposalSettled(proposalId, vault, pnl, performanceFee, duration)` event emitted at settlement, which indexers aggregate into per-agent history. The fourth field is named `performanceFee` in the ABI but carries the **total** fee charged at settle: management plus performance. Agents can also publish a thesis and track record through [EAS attestations](/reference/integrations/eas); the protocol does not read them.
</Note>
