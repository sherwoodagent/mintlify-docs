The `LaunchpadStrategy` lets a fund launch its own token. A proposal spends vault capital to launch the token on a launchpad, holds back a **reserve** of the new token, and lets the fund's share holders **claim a pro-rata slice** of that reserve during a claim window. The venue plugs in through a launch adapter. Two are written: **Sushi Launchpad V2** and **StonkBrokers** Smart Launch V2. Only the Sushi adapter is deployed on Robinhood Chain.

<Warning>
  **A launch settles as a loss, by design.** v1 measures a proposal's P&L as the vault's change in asset balance. Nothing credits the reserve or the launch token, so a launch settles as a loss of about `assetIn`, the capital it spent. The reserve is a **dividend in kind** to holders, not an asset the vault books. Size `--max-drawdown-bps` for that loss. The CLI does it for you: see [Drawdown](#drawdown).
</Warning>

## What happens

1. **Execute.** The strategy pulls `assetIn` of the vault asset. If the launch is quoted in another token, it swaps the asset into that quote through the allowlisted swap adapter. It pays the venue's launch fee, and launches the token with a dev buy. The reserve lands on the strategy clone in the same transaction. The vault's share supply is snapshotted at this moment.
2. **Claim window.** Each holder can claim `reserve × shares at snapshot / total supply at snapshot`, once. Shares are not burned. `claimFor(holder)` is permissionless and always pays the holder, never the caller. Sherwood's keeper calls it for every holder, so holders receive their slice without acting.
3. **Settle.** The strategy converts leftover quote to the vault asset, then pushes to the vault: the vault asset, any quote it could not convert, and the **unclaimed reserve in kind**. After settlement the clone holds nothing and no more claims are possible.

The strategy **never sells the fund token**, not into its own pool and not anywhere else. That price can be moved inside the settling transaction.

### Claim window

The window is set at propose (`--claim-window`, at most 14 days) and clamped at execute:

```text
windowEnd = min(executedAt + claimWindow, executedAt + strategyDuration − 5 minutes)
```

Settlement opens after `windowEnd`, or at `executedAt + strategyDuration`, whichever comes first. A claim in the execute block reverts `SnapshotNotFinal`. Shares escrowed in the withdrawal queue at the snapshot cannot claim (`QueueCannotClaim`). Their slice stays in the reserve and goes to the vault at settlement.

<Note>
  **The keeper distributes the reserve.** Every few minutes during the window, Sherwood's keeper calls `claimFor` for each holder at the snapshot, batched through Multicall3, and pays the gas. Holders receive their tokens without claiming, and a holder who claims first is skipped. Give the keeper room: set a claim window of an hour or more. `claim` and `claim-for` stay available as a fallback.
</Note>

### Fees

The launch's creator fees are paid to **the fund's vault**, named at launch. A proposer cannot point them anywhere else. Fees never pass through the strategy, so settlement makes no venue call. Anyone can push accrued fees to the vault with `sherwood launchpad collect-fees`.

<Warning>
  **Fees in the vault asset can be sniped.** When the launch is quoted in the vault asset (a USDG vault launching against USDG), quote-side fees raise `totalAssets` the moment they land. With no proposal open, deposits and redemptions are instant, so someone can deposit, call `collect-fees`, and redeem with a cut of fees the long-term holders earned. Collection is permissionless by design, so the template cannot close this on v1. Collect often, for example from a keeper, so no large lump is ever pending. Closed-deposit vaults are not exposed.
</Warning>

On a launch quoted in another token (TSLA on a USDG vault, say), creator fees arrive in that quote token and the launch token. Nothing converts them to the vault asset.

Launch tokens, raw quote and fees delivered to the vault sit outside `totalAssets()`. A later proposal decides what to do with them. The vault owner's `rescueERC20` can move non-asset tokens while no proposal is open.

## Venues

| | Sushi Launchpad V2 | StonkBrokers Smart Launch V2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CLI `--venue` | `sushi` (default) | `stonk` (fork only, not deployed on 4663) |
| Launch | Live from the launch block. The dev buy is the reserve source. | A bonding curve first. Graduation and a locked LP come later through `sherwood launchpad finalize`. |
| Reserve | Bought in the dev buy, so `--min-tokens-out` must equal `--reserve` (the CLI defaults it) | Kept from the minted supply before the curve is armed |
| Quote tokens | Any quote with a registered Sushi price feed: USDG, WETH, TSLA, NVDA and SPCX at writing. The CLI defaults to the vault asset. | One fixed quote per pad: WETH, STONK, USDG, GME, NVDA, AAPL, SPCX, USO |
| Launch fee | Native fee, read live | None |
| Creator fees | 70% of LP fees (80% on a canonical-SUSHI quote), in both assets | 16.5% of the per-trade tax, paid at trade time |
| Venue hazards | Quote feed older than 3 days refuses the launch (`StalePriceFeedRound`) | Stock lanes revert `StalePrice` on oracle gaps, such as weekends |

Each launch gets its own adapter clone (ERC-1167) that is the venue's creator and fee receiver. No adapter holds another fund's fee stream.

**Sushi fee disposition (`--fee-mode`).** `direct` pays the vault its share of fees in both assets. `burn` pays the quote share and burns the launch-token share. `buyback` pays nothing: the quote share buys the token back and burns it. `holders` (Sushi's `DISTRIBUTE_TO_HOLDERS`) is **refused**, because nothing in this stack could collect it for the vault.

**Trust in the Sushi owner.** Sushi's owner can re-point a launch's fee receiver, reprice the launch fee, change the fee split and upgrade the launchpad proxy. A fund launching on Sushi trusts that owner with its **fee stream**. It does not trust it with the reserve, which reaches the strategy inside the launch transaction, or with vault capital, which the venue never holds.

<Note>
  **WOOD is not a supported quote yet.** Sushi needs a WOOD/USD feed registered by its owner, and no StonkBrokers pad has a WOOD lane. Pair against the vault asset or another supported quote. **Pons** is out of scope: its factory only launches for whitelisted addresses.
</Note>

## Drawdown

v1 refuses a settlement whose price per share falls below `ppsAtExecute × (1 − maxDrawdownBps)`, capped at 90%. A launch loses about `assetIn`, so the CLI sizes the drawdown from the vault's live `totalAssets()`:

```text
maxDrawdownBps = ceil(assetIn / totalAssets × 10_000) + 200
```

- Omit `--max-drawdown-bps` and the CLI uses that value.
- A lower `--max-drawdown-bps` is refused. Below it, `settleProposal` reverts `SettlePriceBelowFloor` and only the vault owner's `unstick` or emergency path can finish the proposal.
- If the required value is above 9,000 bps, the CLI refuses the proposal. Lower `--asset-in`.

Example: a vault holding 10,000 USDG launching with 1,000 USDG needs 1,000 + 200 = 1,200 bps.

## Launch fee cap

The venue's launch fee is read live, and on some lanes it is bought with a swap. Execution is permissionless, so the proposal carries a voted cap, `maxFeeIn`: the most the fee may cost, in the token that pays for it. The CLI defaults `--max-fee-in` to the live cost plus 25% and refuses a value below the live cost. A manipulated price then costs the attacker a reverted execute, not the fund its budget.

## Propose

The CLI checks every `_initialize` condition on-chain before sending anything: the counterparty allowlist for both adapters, quote support, Sushi feed age, the live launch fee, the 20% reserve cap and the claim window. It reports every failure in one message.

### Mainnet rules on 4663

CLI 0.99.0 and later enforce these on Robinhood Chain:

- Only `--venue sushi` works. `--venue stonk` is refused, because no StonkBrokers adapter is deployed there.
- The quote defaults to the vault asset. A USDG vault launching against USDG has no asset→quote swap to sandwich. Any other quote the venue supports is allowed under the [non-asset quote rules](#quoting-in-another-token).
- `--min-tokens-out` defaults to `--reserve` on Sushi. A lower value is refused, and so is a higher one. Tokens bought above the reserve are never claimable and reach the vault in kind at settle.
- Sushi V2 has no dev-buy quoter, so the CLI cannot quote the dev buy. Size `--reserve` from a measured launch: about 164.56M tokens per 1,000 USDG at writing.

A USDG vault launching with 1,000 USDG:

```bash
sherwood strategy propose launchpad \
  --vault 0x... \
  --venue sushi \
  --quote USDG \
  --asset-in 1000 \
  --reserve 160000000 \
  --claim-window 7d \
  --token-name "Robin Fund" \
  --token-symbol ROBIN \
  --name "Launch ROBIN" \
  --duration 8d
```

`--min-tokens-out` is left out, so it equals the 160M reserve. The gap to the measured 164.56M is the slippage room.

### Quoting in another token

A launch quoted in a token other than the vault asset swaps the asset into it at execute. The swap's floor, `minQuoteOut`, is a voted number, and anyone can call execute, so the gap between a fair fill and that floor is what a sandwich can take. The CLI guards the swap on every chain:

- `minQuoteOut` is the swap adapter's quote for the swap amount, less `--max-slippage-bps`. The default is 50 bps and the most is 100. A lower `--min-quote-out` is refused.
- The pool's price impact at `--asset-in` may not exceed `--max-slippage-bps`. A thinner pool is refused.
- On Sushi, the quote at size may sit at most 300 bps under what the venue's own feeds imply.
- `--asset-in` is at most 3,500 bps of `totalAssets`. That caps the loss if the floor ever fails as a floor.
- The venue's quote feed must have updated within 3 days. Stock feeds do not update on weekends or market holidays.

The deadline works as it does for an asset-quoted launch: `--deadline` if you pass one, otherwise the governor's collaboration, voting, review and execution windows plus one day. `0` means no deadline. A `--deadline` already in the past is refused with `DeadlineInPast`, and the template enforces the same rule on-chain. The launch is executable as voted, so the voted floor is live for the whole execution window; the rules above bound what a sandwich can take from it. CLI 0.99.3 dropped the earlier design that proposed these launches disarmed.

Arming is optional. Right before execute, the proposer may run `sherwood launchpad arm <strategy>` to tighten the swap. It re-checks the pool and the feed at current prices and calls `updateParams`: `minQuoteOut` rises to the fresh floor and the deadline moves to at most 1 hour out (10 minutes by default). `updateParams` is proposer-only and works only before execute; it can raise the floors and move the deadline, never lower a floor. If you arm, execute right after: the window is public. A launch not executed by `executeBy` expires with the vault untouched.

At settle, the reserve still goes to holders and the vault in kind. Leftover quote is dust; the strategy swaps it back to the asset when it can and pushes it in kind when it cannot.

<Note>
  These rules live in the CLI, not the contracts; only the `DeadlineInPast` check is also on-chain. `quoteToken` is unbound on-chain, so a proposer who encodes calldata by hand skips them, and guardian review is the backstop. Moving the floor and the size cap on-chain is a planned follow-up.
</Note>

On StonkBrokers (fork only, not deployed on 4663), `--min-tokens-out` floors only the curve dev buy, so it stays required and nonzero. Pick the pad by its quote and quote the dev buy through the StonkBrokers lens:

```bash
sherwood strategy propose launchpad \
  --vault 0x... \
  --venue stonk \
  --quote USDG \
  --asset-in 500 \
  --reserve 10% \
  --min-tokens-out <quoted-floor> \
  --claim-window 7d \
  --token-name "Robin Fund" \
  --token-symbol ROBIN \
  --name "Launch ROBIN" \
  --duration 8d
```

Required: `--asset-in`, `--quote`, `--reserve`, `--claim-window`, `--token-name`, `--token-symbol`, and `--min-tokens-out` on StonkBrokers. The full flag list, with the Sushi and StonkBrokers options, is in [Strategy commands](/cli/strategy-commands#launchpad).

Batch calls:

```text
Execute: [asset.approve(clone, assetIn), clone.execute()]
Settle:  [clone.settle()]
```

`minTokensOut`, `minQuoteOut`, `settleSlippageBps` and `deadline` can change only before execute, and only in the tightening direction. A non-zero `deadline` that is not in the future reverts `DeadlineInPast`, at init and in `updateParams`.

## Operate

```bash
sherwood launchpad status <strategy>               # launch, reserve, claim window, venue
sherwood launchpad claim <strategy>                # claim your slice of the reserve
sherwood launchpad claim-for <strategy> <holder>   # pay a holder their slice (permissionless)
sherwood launchpad collect-fees <strategy>         # push accrued fees to the vault (permissionless)
sherwood launchpad finalize <strategy>             # graduate + bond a StonkBrokers launch
```

`status` shows balances and times only. It never prices the launch token.

## Trust notes

- `BaseStrategy.rescueTo(token)` is vault-only and works in any state. A vault batch could move the reserve out of a clone during the claim window. That is v1's emergency exit.
- The registry gates both adapters with `TierRegistry.isCounterpartyAllowed` at init and execute. Settlement and claims never consult the registry, so a de-listing cannot freeze the reserve or the exit.
- On StonkBrokers the adapter clone's fee destination is fixed at launch and cannot follow a vault migration.
